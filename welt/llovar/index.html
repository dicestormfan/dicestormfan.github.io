Llovar

Llovar Rutonu Lokander, born in 412, was a prodigiously gifted nightseer of the Loth K'har who fell into irrevocable corruption in the T'mara T'rusheen and forged his people into the Uthuk Y'llan, plunging Mennara into the First Darkness.

Early Life

Llovar's family was noted among the Loth K'har for producing many renowned nightseers and chiefs, from tribespeople able to survive even in the far-eastern wastes where deep volcanic vents usher poisonous fumes from the world's depths; some scholars suspect one of the Daewyl rents between Mennara and the Ynfernael can be found within those eastern wastes, and that Llovar was sensitive to the Ynfernael's influence from infancy. His elder brother Llander showed quality as a raider and forager despite his tender years, but much was expected of young Llovar, and he defied those expectations further still: he quickly became recognized for his prodigious dreamwalking abilities, earning the title of nightseer by his eleventh birthday. He grew increasingly reckless, though, and during a lengthy sojourn in the T'mara T'rusheen, Llovar is said to have met with an aspect of the Ynfernael and fallen into irrevocable corruption. Within the harsh but otherwise peaceful society of the Loth K'har, his prowess and wayward genius was celebrated despite the clear signs of malign influence; the chieftains of his tribe hailed him as a holy figure, then made him their peer, then their leader. Those who opposed his increasing power were shunned or executed, though Llander remained a close confidant despite having voiced misgivings about his brother's rule.

Llovar Ascendant

Llovar sprung his carefully laid scheme during the Night of Summons, leading his tribe's greatest warriors into the T'mara T'rusheen with promises of great rewards and forbidden bindings; they emerged physically transformed by Ynfernael influence, their wiry muscles grown supernaturally powerful and their very bones pushing through their skin to form great spikes and blades. With these warriors at his side, Llovar formed a confederation of tribes, conquering those who refused to ally willingly — even the chiefs of the Scal and L'jan tribes, many times larger than the Loth K'har, bent the knee. His campaign bloomed into full civil war between the tribes of the plains, and the Loth K'har and their allies took on a new title, the Uthuk Y'llan, "locust swarm" in the language of the plains people, as they conquered more and more of The Ru.

By 478, Llovar had finished his consolidation of the northern plains, and the Uthuk Y'llan armies marched to despoil the southern lands, bringing about the First Darkness. He could not have brought war to Talindon alone, however: the Orcs of The Broken Plains were the first to pledge allegiance to the Locust Lord under their bellicose Chief Lukosh, later joined by Warlord Hellspanth and his band of dragons from the far north. Llovar reserved final say on matters of strategy and led the Uthuk atop his beloved steed Dire, a carnivorous beast he had personally summoned from the Ynfernael.

The First Darkness

Throughout 478, Llovar's armies burned the eastern groves of The Aymhelin and plundered Talindon, bettering the forces of King Falladir Penacor in open battle. The following year, Llovar sent his Orc allies raiding while he marched his Uthuk north to besiege Thelgrim, considering the Dwarven capital a particular prize for its command of surrounding supply routes; lacking the equipment to storm it, his forces instead encircled the fortress to starve out its defenders. An alliance of Humans and Elves under Falladir and King Triamlavar relieved the siege, and by 483 Timmorran's campaign, aided by Falladir and Triamlavar, began to turn the tide — at the Battle of the Burning Hills, Timmorran struck down Chief Lukosh, breaking the Orcs' loyalty to Llovar's cause, and by 486 Llovar could no longer call upon a single Orcish regiment. Llovar's own lost legion under the general Waiqar Sumarion, sent by Timmorran directly toward Llovar's Black Citadel in the Charg'r Wastes, vanished from all record for the rest of the war, its path becoming known as the Road of Skulls.

In the distant Ru Darklands, Llovar gathered his reserves, now able to rely only on Warlord Hellspanth's dragons after the loss of his Orc allies; still, the army grown so great it was called the Host of Thorns, split into contingents led by trusted lieutenants — the bone-witch Q'aro Fenn, Melinesh the Cruel and Terrible, Nashaia King's Bane, and Kul the Serpent, the greatest archer of the Uthuk. In 487 the Host of Thorns invaded Talindon, and at The Ashen Field it fell upon an army led by Falladir and his sons, slaying all three Penacors and their soldiers; only a small band of survivors led by the young Baron Daqan escaped, warning Timmorran of Llovar's renewed force. Timmorran made his stand in the foothills of the Razor Crags, reinforced unexpectedly by Dwarven troops from Thelgrim under Halgir Son of Grom. At the Battle of the Locusts, Kul's assault nearly broke Timmorran's position, wounding Triamlavar, killing Halgir, and killing Ashan the Elder, but Timmorran had conserved his own magical energies for the final clash — he boiled away Llovar's summoned Ynfernael monstrosities with blazing arcane light, withstood the Locust Lord's ensorcelled blade behind a shield of pure force, and at last pierced his uncle's rotten heart with a lance of arcane power. Dire fell alongside his master, his body dissolving as its energies returned to the Ynfernael, and the Host of Thorns turned and fled; the First Darkness had passed.

Death and Legacy

In the ensuing chaos, the body of the Locust Lord was lost amid looting and disorganized pursuit, and the fate of his lieutenants was never confirmed. Some say Q'aro Fenn rescued Llovar's corpse from the crags and took it to a secret place far beyond the Ru, a resting place said to be found only by following the trail of her bitter tears; when Llovar was slain, his lieutenants slaughtered every last Uthuk who knew of the Black Citadel's location, before sacrificing themselves in one last act of loyalty to their master, so that the Black Citadel disappeared from history, its wealth and secrets hidden by sorcery somewhere deep in the Darklands.

Timmorran later journeyed with an army into the Charg'r Wastes to rescue Waiqar Sumarion, discovering the Black Citadel and vanquishing the horrors that dwelt therein along with the forlorn general, and — inadvertently — releasing the ancient Elf Zarihell as well. The Flayed Kindred, descended from Llovar's inner circle, hold many of the keys believed to open the Black Citadel's gates, and many of their witches and warlocks believe that if it can be found, Llovar himself might be returned to the world. The nightseer Quin'tar Shymaex seeks to transform all Uthuk into true demons, a goal that eluded even Llovar himself. The corrupted Elven tribe of the Daewyl also drew on the same Ynfernael power, in their covetous pursuit of Latariana's Tears, while Maegan Cyndewin's mother of the Loth Caara lived to see Llovar corrupt her own people in turn.
