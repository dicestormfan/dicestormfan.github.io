Kell

Kell, the Shield of Daqan, lies along the Dunwarr mountain range that shields Terrinoth from the foreboding Charg'r Wastes, its southern end abutting the Borderlands that separate Terrinoth from the Ru.

Due to its border position, Kell maintains one of the largest and most heavily trained armies of all the baronies, fed by a fertile river valley and based out of numerous ancient keeps; in the depths of several keeps, magnificent rune golems await the day they are woken and called to war once more, though most outside Kell take its protection for granted and rarely consider the Ru a source of threat. Kell's soldiers watch the Ru's desolation and the fords of the Lothan River at Hernfar Isle from ancient, crumbling watchtowers whose builders are long forgotten. Behind these timeworn fortresses, Kell's citizens prosper: valley farms keep the barony fed with enough surplus to trade with the nearby Dunwarr and Joulnar Dwarves and the barony of Dhernas, and a rich trade in timber and iron flows into Baron Fredric's vaults, seldom resting long before he puts it toward the betterment of his people. It is said that Roland of Kell slew High Lord Margath at the culmination of the Dragon Wars, and many see that nobility in his descendant. Baroness Elyana of Kell, in the last years of the 1500s, restored the Council of Thirteen after a generation of famine and baronial neglect.

Not far from Castle Kellar stands Orrush Khatak, generally called the Gate of the Furnace — a great arch of blood red rock large enough for four hundred soldiers to march under shoulder to shoulder, its origin as natural formation, luck charm, or ritual site now lost to memory, and one where the wind is said to carry anguished cries few will admit to being more than a trick of the breeze. Beyond it runs the wide river Lothan, and Hernfar Isle, the furthest eastern outpost of the Daqan Lords, sits where the river is widest; recruits joke that insubordination in the Kell militia earns assignment to the garrison at Hernfar Keep. The isle seems unremarkable but for its proximity to the Ru Steppes, and soldiers posted there report shadows that seem to lengthen or flicker, luminescent eyes staring from across the water, and on nights when the moon is heavy and red, a cacophony of howls and screams that few can confidently call merely animal.
