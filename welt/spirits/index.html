Spirits

No one knows for certain from whence the spirits of Mennara came, or how they relate to the dragons that created the world. Some scholars theorize that spirits are the collection of magical energies from the Turning in specific locations, which eventually coalesce into intelligent beings; others postulate that spirits spilled over from the Aenlong and made their residence in the Firma Dracem; still others believe they are accidental side effects from the moment when the Yrthwright Mennara created the world.

Description

All that is known with certainty is that spirits dwell in beautiful and wondrous places all across Mennara, congregating most strongly in wild areas far from cities and towns — perhaps because they are most likely to inhabit ancient trees and misty caves, the very things cleared or avoided when settlements are built, or perhaps simply because spirits do not care for the mortal races. Confusion exists over the distinction between the spirits of the land and the wraiths that haunt places stained by anger, despair, or violence; the spirits found in the wilds are seen more as avatars or emissaries of the natural world, whether of a particular place or of a particular object or organism, as with tree spirits such as the one said to reside in the Tree of Tales. Spiritspeakers are believed able to commune with restless ghosts as well, but no one has tried to speak with the specters that plague the Mistlands.

Those with the gift — or perhaps simply the patience — to communicate with spirits are called spiritspeakers, taught to awaken or seek the spirits' attention through chanting, drums, dance, and offerings of gifts or service. Once they have gained a spirit's ear, they may ask for boons such as healing or luck in a particular endeavor, or even ask a spirit to visit harm upon an enemy who has aggrieved it as well; spirits can also empower those who speak with them with a portion of their magic, enabling the spiritspeaker to fight with the power of flame, wind, stone, or water. Spiritspeaking is in some ways similar to dreamwalking, in that spirits can share their knowledge with those who commune with them — more reliable than dreams, but also more limited, for a spirit's answers are restricted to what it has itself learned or heard from other spirits.

Spiritspeaking is one of the oldest forms of magic, arising from the need of the earliest peoples to coexist alongside the tumultuous landscape and the spirits that dwelled there. As the ages passed, the arcane arts eventually supplanted spiritspeaking in many cultures, for wizards need not cajole or appease another entity to create desired effects. In cultures that still live in close connection with the land, however, such as the Orc tribes of the Broken Plains, the Singhara prides of Zanaga, the Onoit Gnomes of Isheim, and many far-flung human settlements, spiritspeaking remains a tremendously important part of daily life and survival. Most spiritspeakers seem rather eccentric and strange, with a touch of the wild about them, whether from living in seclusion to be closer to spirits, from the spirits' own eccentric influence, or simply from a wisdom gained by learning more of the world than those who look down on them.
