Daqan

King Daqan founded the kingdom of Terrinoth, which has stood for more than thirteen centuries since, largely ruled since his time by a Council of Barons.

History

As a young man, Baron Daqan led a small group of ragged survivors from The Ashen Field, where the Host of Thorns had slain three generations of Penacors together with their soldiers during the First Darkness, escaping the slaughter to warn Timmorran of Llovar's renewed force. In the war's aftermath, with Talindon's Penacor line extinguished, the Lords of Talindon suggested Daqan accept the vacant crown; Timmorran, hoping to install a new form of government inspired by lands he had visited, persuaded him to take the throne on the condition that he swear to have no heirs and instead install a Council of Barons to govern after him. Whether Daqan exercised his own will in this or merely acted as Timmorran's puppet is a matter of scholarly debate, though many hold that the two men simply shared the same vision. His coronation was held in 488, and Daqan was soon regarded as a confident and self-assured ruler.

Founding Terrinoth

Shortly after taking the throne, Timmorran assembled an army and traveled deep into the Charg'r Wastes to discover the fate of Waiqar Sumarion, eventually finding and rescuing him from Llovar's Black Citadel; upon his return, Timmorran urged Daqan to forbid travel east into those lands and had every map depicting the Charg'r Wastes destroyed. Once Waiqar recovered, Daqan sent him to command the Ru Campaign against the Uthuk warlord Da'Roul Bonesplicer, ending the threat of resurgent Uthuk for generations.

As Daqan organized the rebuilding of the kingdom, Elves traveled the land openly to assist in the rehabilitation of rural communities, and Dwarven engineers from Dunwarr directed the mining of rich iron deposits in the northern hills — a cosmopolitan flavor, together with the increasing authority of the Council of Barons, that led to a new age of optimism and prosperity. Daqan, perhaps at Timmorran's urging, declared himself unworthy of the Penacor name and proposed that the realm of Talindon pass into history with its rulers, taking instead the new name Terrinoth, "Land of Steel." He never occupied the Penacor keep in Tamalir, instead drafting a charter granting the city's elders both the keep and its own governance, provided they abided by the Council's majority decisions, tithed a portion of their taxes, and kept a standing force for their own protection and the realm's — so it was that Tamalir became the first of the Free Cities. Daqan also founded the great citadel of Archaut in a calm southern valley, placing at its center a great hall where, at the beginning of every spring, the barons met to accept his hospitality and discuss matters of the realm; it was during one such gathering that Waiqar Sumarion was elevated to the Council and granted the thirteenth barony in the northwest of Terrinoth, near The Misty Hills and the marshes of the Karahesh. Not everyone was pleased with Daqan's reforms — Baron Ulon Heronglade in particular held reactionary opinions regarding kingship, and his opposition to Daqan often resulted in heated arguments.

Death

In 513, King Daqan traveled to the southwestern reaches of Terrinoth to arbitrate a border dispute between two barons that had developed into a series of skirmishes. He never arrived: his crowned helmet was discovered near a lonely stretch of road, his party seemingly ambushed and slaughtered, their remains scattered by wolves. In the cities of Terrinoth, it was whispered that the Bloodguard Knights had been hired to waylay and murder the king by none other than Baron Ulon Heronglade. Led by Waiqar, the Council took resolute action: it had Castle Heronglade razed to the ground and the baron seized, executed in a cage hung from the gates of Archaut despite his protests of innocence to the last. Some scholars argue that Waiqar Sumarion, who soon used the ensuing chaos to unleash evil upon Terrinoth, may have been the true culprit rather than Heronglade — though others note that Timmorran, too, stood to gain from Daqan's death, as opposition to the Council of Barons grew markedly more circumspect in its aftermath. As Daqan's body was never recovered, some hold hope that he was not killed at all, and that his return would herald a new golden age.

Legacy

Terrinoth is largely ruled through the annual gathering of the Daqan Lords, the barons descended from Daqan's era, at the Citadel in Archaut. Though only twelve barons attend in the present day — the thirteenth barony having been lost over a millennium ago to the treachery of Waiqar — the gathering is still often called the "Council of Thirteen," whether out of respect for King Daqan or as a reminder of the cost of betrayal. His castle became known as the Citadel, the administrative and judicial heart of Archaut, and his badge — a gold crown on a blue field — remains the heraldry worn by the Citadel's servants to this day. The Statue of Daqan in Archaut, greatest of the city's many monuments, remains as unblemished today as the day it was erected, as if the elements themselves defer to the lost king.
