Dawnsmoor

Dawnsmoor is one of the eight Free Cities of Terrinoth, founded by refugees during the Second Darkness, a city of mixed groups including mercenary companies, nomadic tribes, traveling caravans, and most notably a large Elven population.

As Waiqar's Deathborn Legion scourged Terrinoth and life in the cities grew perilous, citizens fled in droves, and a number of refugees set up a rough camp by the banks of the Flametail River — its position was advantageous, allowing supplies from Lorimor to easily reach the camp, and it soon gained the name Dawnsmoor, perhaps as the refugees began to see some glimmer of hope for their future. It was here that a stranger calling himself Kellos first arrived by boat, working miracles of healing among the refugees before becoming the central figure of a burgeoning cult that would grow into the Church of Kellos.

Despite its relatively small size, Dawnsmoor's location on the Flametail River gives it excellent access to trade and a water route east to nearby Tamalir; the river has become something of an extension of the city itself, and many of its workers return to riverside homes each nightfall. Its rowers and fisherfolk are among the best in Terrinoth, and many a deepwater ship captain started as a Dawnsmoor river rat. Though many of the city's peoples come and go, its Elves retain a steady presence, and Elven culture has become embedded within Dawnsmoor, giving it a warm relationship with the Latari and some of the finest archers in Terrinoth — though the relationship between the city's Elves and Humans is not always smooth, with frequent trade disputes and jurisdiction conflicts.

Lacking city walls, Dawnsmoor has become a waypoint for less-than-savory characters seeking rest or coin, and along with its largely itinerant populace this has given it a reputation as a place where almost anything can be purchased or contracted, no matter the legality. Some folk have relocated to smaller villages around the city, on the river, or on the moors of the Misty Plains to the south, where settlements like Brightvale spring up and slowly consolidate into hamlets, though even there an undercurrent of the disreputable can be found.
