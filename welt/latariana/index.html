Latariana

Lady Latariana, called Mother to the Elves and Lady Protectress of Air, was the wife of Emorial whose covetous desire for greater power set in motion the Elves' rebellion against The First and their exile to Mennara.

History

The First undertook the creation of Lord Emorial and Lady Latariana together, prominent among the more familiar beings born from the Empyrean spheres: he the Lord Protector of Light, she the Lady Protectress of Air. It is not entirely clear whether the two were themselves the literal progenitors of the Elven people or merely the first of them to be created and gain particular recognition, but certainly they were lovers, wedded in the fashion of their folk, and soon the Elves numbered in legions.

As the Verto Magica turned and time passed, Latariana slowly began to covet more than her existence in the Empyrean, longing for a Crown of Dreams and Life — greater power than the Elves possessed in that plane. Scholars ponder over why she became avaricious; consensus suggests a shadowy spirit from the Ynfernael tempted her in secret murmurs, though others suggest the First, lacking personality, were simply too cold a presence for Elves to form any deep relationship with. Whatever her motives, she cajoled her husband with ceaseless requests to win her dominion over the primary spheres, or perhaps all the spheres, of the Empyrean. Emorial eventually agreed, traveling to the farthest edge of creation to demand the First relinquish control; they did not so much as deign to respond, leaving him humiliated and wrathful. He returned to the Elves and marshaled them in mutiny, marching his host with Latariana at his side through The Gate of Years — the Elves believed they would pass into the halls of the First, but instead found themselves crossing into the Aenlong, and after countless days lost among its hazards, they came at last to the glade that would become The Aymhelin, where they met its architect, the Yrthwright Mennara.

The great dragon passed the judgments of the First down upon the rebellious Elves: they were exiled from the Empyrean, Light and Air no longer theirs to command, the Lays of the First no longer freely nourishing their hearts, and — the cruelest judgment of all — they were now imprisoned upon the very mortal plane that had been both their destination and their damnation, this world that some dare name after the Yrthwright of this tale.

The Tears of Latariana

The Elves begged Mennara for mercy, but it would not heed them; instead it turned to Latariana and offered her a terrible choice. A window to the Empyrean could be left open for the Elves to return through one day, but the cost was high: Latariana would have to enter the Void, taking with her the Ynfernael temptation that had led to her greed. With a heavy heart, she agreed, whispering her last tender words to Emorial before climbing onto the Yrthwright's back; the majestic creature spread its wings and flew her up into the darkening skies. The Elves watched as she was borne from the world into the Void, and as she grew too distant to see, a bright light shone in her place — the first star. More stars appeared thereafter, reminders of Latariana's sacrifice, still watched by her followers today from The Ailatar, the clearing where the Elves first descended to Mennara.

Emorial could not look away; staring at his hands, wet with Latariana's tears, he watched them transform before his eyes into eleven stunning pearlescent jewels, mere echoes of his lost love's beauty — tokens of hope, like the stars, that the Elves might one day earn forgiveness. Wracked with shame, anger, and grief he could not accept for himself, Emorial called forth eleven Elven nobles and divided his host into eleven tribes, sharing between them the Tears of Latariana as marks of office, directing the nobles to lead their people into the future of this new world until a path back to the Empyrean was made clear. He then took up his sword and, with cold fury in his heart, left his people — the Elves say he journeyed to the Ynfernael plane to seek vengeance on the corrupting shadow that had murmured temptations to his bride.

Two centuries after their arrival, the eleven tribes scattered to far-flung territories and developed distinct cultures, though they kept links of trade and friendship; only the Latari Elves remained in the forested clearing of the Aymhelin. Among the eleventh tribe, the Malcari, the bearer of a Tear named Malcorne, Lord of the Daewyl Tribe, would in time corrupt his own Tear into the Shadow Tear and plunge the Elves into the Shadow War (also called The War of the Shadow Tear) — the Shadow Tear went missing during that war and, if it remains unbroken, a powerful artifact combining both Empyrean and Ynfernael magic still waits somewhere for someone to find it. Even the surviving Tears of Latariana, guarded within Caelcira to this day, were not immune to the corruption of the Ynfernael, and an old adage among the Eolam court holds that Latariana's tears actually fall again due to the innumerable intrigues, plots, and jealousies that plague the court.

In their original form, the Tears were crystallized Empyrean energy, and are believed to be artifacts that anticipated the form and function of the Stars of Timmorran in many ways — great batteries of energy that could conceivably power hugely potent spells. Being only eleven in number, they are even rarer than the few unbound Stars that remain in the world; each is kept carefully guarded by the leader of its tribe as both a potent magical artifact and a revered badge of office, and while an Elven lord might on occasion allow a Tear to be used in some great working for the benefit of all Mennara, none would ever trust its use to someone from outside the tribes.

Worship

Latariana is the guiding light of the Latari Elves, visible among the other glimmering lights in the night sky from The Ailatar. Her followers venerate her through the Path to the Stars.

The Highborn Elves — those only slightly removed from the most ancient bloodlines who dwelled in the Empyrean — hold the strongest natural connection to Latariana's Door, and many devote their lives to deepening that connection and ascending Latariana's Stair in hope of earning redemption for all Elvenkind. The Daughters of the Empyrean and the Darnati warriors are most often drawn from this caste.
