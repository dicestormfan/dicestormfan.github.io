Empyrean

The Empyrean is the highest of the three Planes of Power The First conjured from the Void, a plane of perfection where the cares of hunger, age, and discomfort are utterly unknown. It is the place the Latari Elves eternally quest to return to; all Latari, wherever they dwell in The Aymhelin, fiercely guard their forests from outside intrusion lest contaminations disrupt that quest.

Description

Elven legends speak of four spheres existing in the Empyrean in unadulterated form: light, air, life, and dreams. The Elves themselves once dwelt wholly within the Empyrean, their spirits nourished through their relation to these spheres, until Emorial and Latariana's rebellion saw them exiled from it and imprisoned upon the mortal plane. Some mages believe Ynfernael magic to be a dark counterpart to Divine magic, holding that as the Empyrean blesses all that is good and pure in magic, so the Ynfernael empowers that which is horrid and dark.

While human sorcerers pull magical energies from the Verto Magica, the Elves draw upon the Empyrean instead. As a result of Latariana's repentance and sacrifice, the stars in the night sky came into existence, the first of which, according to Elven tradition, provides a tiny but open window — Latariana's Door — into the Empyrean; a steady stream of Empyrean energy filters through this window and helps nourish the mortal realms. The Elves are uniquely attuned to such energies, which their priests can access and work into miraculous feats by following the traditions of the Path to the Stars. All four spheres are believed to have been utilized by the Yrthwrights in the creation of Mennara; according to Elven myths, Emorial and Latariana were once granted dominion over light and air respectively, and Elven priests find it much easier to access energies from these two spheres compared with those of life and dreams.

Before their Fall, the original pair of Elves embodied light and air, and through Latariana's Door their descendants still have access to these spheres — learning spells that affect light and air comes very easily to the Elves, and even those who do not devote themselves completely to the Elven priesthood are able to create or extinguish small globes of light or conjure up a faint breeze. Subtle differences exist between spells that manipulate existing light or air in the Firma Dracem and those that summon light or air from the Empyrean itself: illuminating a darkened corner requires only that the Elf redirect light from one part of a room to another, a relatively minor feat, while conjuring daylight in an otherwise pitch-black cavern means summoning light directly from the Empyrean, requiring far greater strength of will and concentration.

The stuff of life and dreams were greatly coveted by the Elves in the time before their Fall — Latariana's desire for power over those two spheres drove Emorial to commit his great folly. Although the Elves do not have the same connection to life and dreams as they did to light and air, Elves who join the priesthood of the Starlight Council are taught the mysteries of the remaining two spheres. With study and practice, their healers may tap into powerful life magic to perform feats of mending rarely seen outside the boughs of The Aymhelin: flesh wounds knit closed effortlessly and painlessly, broken bones come together once more as a solid whole, illnesses of body and mind can be assuaged, and unsettled emotions may be calmed, all fear and anger taken away.

The dreamers among the priesthood spend most of their time in slumber, using their dreams as a portal to the Aenlong. There they may walk between worlds and catch glimpses of that which was, is, and may be; through the Aenlong as a bridge, they may also cross to the far corners of Mennara to touch the minds of other dreamers and discover their hopes, fears, and desires, working to prevent conflicts before they begin or know the precise moment to strike. Once they have tasted of the dream, however, few dreamers can bring themselves to return to the waking world for long, and most spend their eternal lives slumbering under the glimmering white ceilings of the Temple to the Stars. Since the War of the Shadow Tear, parts of the sphere of dreams have become tainted with Ynfernael energies, and on occasion a dreamer will succumb to the nightmare; the Elven high priestesses believe this is connected with the rifts between the Aenlong, the Ynfernael, and Mennara that were opened by Malcorne and his tribesmen. The Elves are not the only people to have learned to walk in dreams: long ago, the shamans of the Loth Caara were known for the realm of T'mara T'rusheen, or "walking in-between," which they may have learned from Elves who mingled with them; it is believed this in-between realm was the very same walked by the Elves in their dreams, and now the nightseers of the Uthuk Y'llan tribes stalk this realm with their own corrupted version of the T'mara T'rusheen, using the Aenlong as a place to cavort with demons before summoning them from the Ynfernael into the world.

A human sorcerer wishing to create a gust of wind would simply summon forth energy from the Verto Magica, yet for an Elven wizard to do the same, they would need to summon forth a degree of air, and then a small degree of life to propel the gust; a more complex spell, equivalent in effect to a fireball created by a human sorcerer, would require input from three spheres — air to fuel the spell, light to imbue it with fire, and life to propel the missile. The more complex the spell, the more spheres must be involved in its creation. Certain magical processes that true sorcerers can undertake are virtually impossible to produce using Empyrean magic, especially those spells which directly influence water and stone — Elven priests can only accomplish such feats through indirect means, such as summoning gusts of air to blow down rocks. Illusionary magic, however, comes easily to Elven wizards: any vision can be conjured from the sphere of dreams, and by combining such visions with energies from the spheres of light and life, such illusions can even become animated and, in extreme cases, capable of interacting with the real world.
