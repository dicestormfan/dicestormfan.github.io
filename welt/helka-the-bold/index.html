Helka the Bold

Helka the Bold is the legendary mother of the Dwarves and first of the runescribes, a skilled miner who traded her sight for the secrets of the dragon runes to save her dying daughter Valnir, and who led the Dwarven armies against the dragons in the last stand at Black Ember Gorge.

History

When the rents left by the Shadow Tear spilled otherworldly energies across the world, the Dwarves are said to have sprung forth from the rocky, flame-wreathed crags of The Molten Heath, scraping out a miserable existence amongst the basalt outcroppings and lava flows of the Heath's heart, hunting the Salamanders that basked along the shores of the molten lakes and foraging for shar-roots and lindengrass beneath the rocks. Amongst them lived Helka, who struggled to raise her daughter Valnir alone after the death of her mate. When Valnir grew sick from the ash-laden skies and all knew she would soon die, Helka refused to accept this, journeying with her daughter deep into the heart of the Heath to find the great and terrible beings that made their lairs in the tallest fire-mountains and demand their aid.

The journey was long and perilous, but finally Helka found a vast cave atop the tallest mountain, within which dwelled a monstrous serpent with silver scales and golden eyes. Fearlessly, she faced the dragon and demanded it help save her daughter's life. The dragon gestured to claw-carved runes covering the cave walls: it would let her use them to save her daughter, but in turn it would take Helka's sight, so that she could not pass that knowledge on to others. The Dwarf agreed, and the dragon showed her which runes could be spoken to heal her child — they would be the last thing she saw, as its wondrous visage grew in brilliance and seared her eyes. The dragon left, and Helka spoke the words of power to cure Valnir; then, feeling her way to the cave wall, she began memorizing by touch the shapes of the other runes carved there. When Helka returned to her kin, she scratched every rune she could remember onto a slab of basalt, and as the Dwarves began to unlock these secrets, the days of their primitive existence ended.

The runes contained the secrets of magic, metalworking, farming, and masonry. With this knowledge, the Dwarves traveled from the fire-scorched heart into the green prairies encircling the Heath, building great cities of brass and basalt they called The Karok Doum, "gift of dragons" in the Dwarven tongue. For many years they worked to build a realm of stability and peace, as Helka continued to study the secrets of the runes and Valnir became a great leader of her people, but the peace was not to last.

The War of Fire

The dragons of the Molten Heath grew to resent the Dwarves, calling them thieves who had stolen draconic knowledge, and eventually they went to war. Though the Dwarves fought valiantly, such a war could only have one outcome; one by one their cities burned, and the shattered remnants of their armies made a final stand at Black Ember Gorge, refugee caravans fleeing behind them into the hills and plains beyond while the dragon armies circled before them. Though old and frail, Helka led a final army against the dragons, telling Valnir to lead the remainder of their people to safety; at the last, Helka unleashed her most devastating runes to destroy the pass along with many dragons, though the rune magic also took the lives of Helka and the brave Dwarves who stood with her that final day.

The Dwarves found no refuge in the rolling, treeless hills and steep ravines that bordered the Molten Heath to the southwest. Eventually their wanderings led them to a vast mountain chain: some journeyed east, forming the settlements of the Jornall Clan, while others continued traveling south, building the city of Yrthwright's Forge beside a volcano in the heart of a bountiful valley, named as a reminder of the glory of their former kingdom. Valnir and many of her people, however, built homes amongst the mist-shrouded peaks that would become the Dunwarr Mountains, driven underground by the occasional dragon still flying out of the north until they left the sky behind entirely and called their new home the Dunwarr, or "sanctuary." The children of Valnir became the first Deeplords, protecting and leading their people.
