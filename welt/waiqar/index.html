Waiqar

Waiqar, later known as Waiqar the Betrayer or Waiqar the Undying, was once the famed Sunderland general Waiqar Sumarion, a trusted ally of Timmorran during the First Darkness, before his fall into undeath and treachery cost Terrinoth its thirteenth barony.

The First Darkness

In 483, during the war against Llovar, Timmorran split his allied army into two contingents, sending Waiqar Sumarion — a proficient wizard as well as a skilled military leader — to relieve the besieged fortress of Thelgrim, a task he accomplished with ease. Growing bored with the duty, Waiqar devised his own campaign against the undead forces of Farrenghast and banished the undead horde in a great success. Eager to press his advantage after the allies' subsequent victory at the Battle of the Burning Hills, Waiqar gathered a warhost and led it into the wastes of the Charg'r, directly toward Llovar's Black Citadel, and vanished from all record for the rest of the war. It was only after King Daqan's coronation that Timmorran assembled an army and journeyed nearly six months into the Charg'r Wastes to discover his fate, finding him at last in a deep oubliette among Llovar's many prisoners — a once-proud general reduced to a forlorn, emaciated figure. Little is known of the details of the rescue itself, save that Timmorran urged Daqan to forbid all further travel east and had every map of the Charg'r Wastes destroyed.

The Ru Campaign

Waiqar's recuperation took many months, and the signs of emaciation and neglect never left him; once confident and brash, he was now given to sullen moods. When Timmorran beseeched him to command the army once again, Waiqar accepted to the rejoicing of his troops, though his taste for recklessness had been wrung from him — he now favored a colder approach to warfare. He commenced the Ru Campaign against the Uthuk warlord Da'Roul Bonesplicer, vanquishing him and annihilating the Uthuk camps throughout the Ru so thoroughly that the threat was believed destroyed for generations. Yet in the hour of his triumph, Waiqar scorned all praise, complaining to his captains of Timmorran's complacency and arrogance — an early sign, in hindsight, of the resentment curdling within him. He was later elevated to the Council of Barons and granted the thirteenth barony in the northwest of Terrinoth, near The Misty Hills and the marshes of the Karahesh, where he ruled justly if with a militaristic bent, training a legion of veteran Sundermen who had settled his lands.

The Night of Betrayal

The motives behind Waiqar's betrayal are known only to himself, though it is popularly supposed that he had been turned against Timmorran and civilized people while incarcerated and tortured in Llovar's dungeons, a darkness that finally bloomed into full hatred and violence. Waiqar gathered troops to his barony, claiming that only he could be trusted with the power of Timmorran's Orb of the Sky. As twilight fell on a cool autumn day in 515, a legion led by Waiqar launched an assault on the Wizard's Vale, storming the Tower of Meringyr with an iron-bound ram, slaying the guards, and putting Timmorran's apprentices to the sword. As Waiqar's warriors neared the workshop, Timmorran — realizing his folly in creating the Orb — shattered it and had Lumii Tamar spirit its fragments away; exhausted from the effort, he could not resist when Waiqar burst in and slew his old friend, tearing the one surviving fragment from his dead hands.

Waiqar vowed to hunt down the remaining fragments, swearing that anyone who denied him so much as the tiniest sliver would face his murderous wrath. Such was the dire weight of that oath, and perhaps so volatile the magical energies unleashed by the Orb's destruction, that his very vow summoned a great tempest of freezing, poisonous rain that snuffed out the lives of all who stood within sight of Meringyr — yet the dead did not rest easy. Waiqar Sumarion, now Waiqar the Undying, left the Wizard's Vale at the head of a fearful column of shambling corpses, the first of his Deathborn Legion.

The Second Darkness

Waiqar returned to his lands, thereafter known as the Cursed Barony, which darkened further still into the dense, dank fog that shrouds it to this day. His agent Zarihell established the first Death Cults of Waiqar in Tamalir, sparking a destructive riot that drew the barons' armies away from his own borders and let him seize the initiative; while his legions plundered towns and farmsteads for weapons and raw materials for new undead, Waiqar directed them to take specific captives back to his lands for interrogation about the Stars of Timmorran. His two chief lieutenants — Zarihell, who organized cult activity, and Ardus Ix'Erebus, who directed the Legion's movements on the field — coordinated their efforts so that every major action of the Deathborn Legion occurred in concert with riots in Terrinoth's cities, ravaging the Barony of Camford to destruction after the Battle of Ramscrossing and scourging the land for six years. Waiqar might have taken Archaut itself and the Stars kept there by the newly formed Lumii Order, but the city proved too well defended and resistant to the cults' influence; only the arrival of the mysterious holy man Kellos at the refugee camp of Dawnsmoor, and the alliance of Elves, Dwarves, and Kellos's followers he helped assemble, finally forced the Deathborn Legion to retreat to the Cursed Barony in 521.

Later Betrayals

His treachery cost Terrinoth its thirteenth barony, a loss so significant that the gathering of the remaining twelve Daqan Lords is still called the "Council of Thirteen" in its memory, and his black chair rests in Archaut as a constant reminder to the barons of the insidious lure of darkness. As Waiqar the Undying, he commands the Deathborn Legion and the undead of his Cursed Barony from the fortress of Zorgas, and spent years searching for the Stars of Timmorran. During the Third Darkness, he dispatched columns of undead warriors to aid the invading dragons — motives that remain disputed, whether to curry favor for a Star of Timmorran, seek revenge on the barons who once humiliated him, or, as some believe, to have engineered the dragon war himself by planting word of the Stars across the Molten Heath to draw Margath's attention. In the Age of Courage, he charged a loyal follower known as the Raven Priest with destroying the Citadel once and for all, arming him with the Carrion Fetish, a talisman infused with the bone dust of dozens of Reanimates; the resulting Raven Horde was destroyed at Carum's Cross by the allied counties of Allerfeldt, Rostum, and Haverford.
