Thelgrim

Thelgrim is the Dwarven capital of the Dunwarr kingdoms, a fortified city built within a vast underground cavern deep in the Dunwarr Mountains.

Overview

Reached through the fortified Gates of Thelgrim and the seven-league Hearth Road, Thelgrim occupies a single vast cavern lit by crystal starglobes, its buildings arranged in rings around a central lake. The Dwarven king or queen rules from Dunwol Kenn Karnin on one shore, while the Guild Hall of the ten civic guilds stands on the other; the city is also home to the Runeforge, seat of the Runescribes' Guild and keeper of the Dwarves' dragon runes, and to the Hall of the Ancestors, resting place of the Dwarven dead.

Founded as a refugee camp during the Years of Exile, Thelgrim was besieged twice by Llovar's Uthuk Y'llan during the First Darkness and relieved both times, before being overrun outright by the Dragonlord Gehennor's forces during the Third Darkness. Its long reconstruction in the Age of Courage, led by First Speaker Ingunn Vergsbold, cemented the political dominance of the Dunwarr Guilds that persists to this day.

History

Thelgrim began as a simple intersection of tunnels in one of the larger caverns of the Dunwarr Deeps, where refugees of the Years of Exile established a series of camps; over time cloth tents gave way to stone walls, and the camp grew into the fragile beginnings of the city that stands today.

During the First Darkness, Llovar marched his Uthuk Y'llan north through the Dunwarr Mountains to besiege Thelgrim, considering the Dwarven capital a particular prize for the position it commanded over the region's supply routes and northern approaches; lacking siege equipment, his forces instead encircled the fortress to starve out its defenders. Falladir Penacor, fighting to hold his own lands against the Uthuk, knew that if Llovar took Thelgrim he would return to ravage Penacor territory next. An alliance of Humans and Elves under Falladir Penacor and King Triamlavar eventually relieved the fortress, though the Dunwarr Deeplords — despite promising the continued use of Thelgrim to their allies — refused to commit their own troops to the field beyond self-defense, over the objections of the Deeplord Halgir Son of Grom. Thelgrim was besieged a second time soon after, and was relieved again when Timmorran sent the Sunderland general Waiqar Sumarion to break the siege. Later in the war, Halgir Son of Grom finally persuaded the Deeplords to commit Dwarven troops, and a force from Thelgrim arrived unexpectedly to reinforce Timmorran's army as it made its stand against Llovar's Host of Thorns at the Battle of the Locusts, which ended the First Darkness with Llovar's death.

Centuries later, during the Third Darkness, the dragon armies of the Dragonlord Gehennor swept along the Hearth Road and overran Thelgrim outright — unlike the Aymhelin, which the Elves managed to defend, Caelcira never falling even as the city took heavy losses. It was in the aftermath of this destruction, deep in the Age of Courage, that the reconstruction of Thelgrim began: Ingunn Vergsbold, then head of the Masons' Guild, set out to rebuild the city's long-destroyed districts. Her work continued for decades, and she did not stop at reconstruction — calling on what remained of Thelgrim's noble wealth, she had the Miners' Guild cut the great chasm linking Thelgrim to the Hearth Road that leads to the outside world, while the Merchants' Guild partnered with the Miners' and Explorers' Guilds to reestablish ties with the other Dunwarr holds. As trade and travel along the reopened Hearth Road increased, so did the power of the guilds; many of the noble families who had refused to invest in the rebuilding lost their estates in Thelgrim to the guilds' expansion, and are said to still plot in distant holds for a chance at revenge. By 1550 the Guilds ruled Dunwarr in all but name, and within two more decades had codified the laws governing the election of Dunwarr's speakers from among the Guildmasters — with Ingunn Vergsbold becoming First Speaker.

Description

Thelgrim is reached through the Gates of Thelgrim, banded and reinforced steel doors nearly two hundred hands high, shaped in the silhouettes of two mighty Dwarven warriors standing back to back. The doors bear the dents and scars of dozens of assaults, testament to their indestructibility, and are backed by hidden defenses: murder holes and disguised firing ports that let unseen Dwarven guardians rake attackers with crossbows, ballistae, and firebombs, hidden sally ports for counterassaults, and a diverted waterfall that can flood the entire approach valley. A light garrison holds the Gates themselves, for even a few score soldiers can hold them for weeks while Thelgrim is only a day's march away; the garrison is commanded by Bradha the Shield, a grim and experienced veteran who also inspects the caravans traveling up from Frostgate to trade with Thelgrim's merchants.

Beyond the Gates lies a vast stone receiving hall, lit by shafts that bring light from the surface, from which the Hearth Road departs: an underground passage seven leagues long and wide enough for five wagons to travel abreast, its walls covered in intricate runework recording a history of the Dwarven people for those who can read it. The road is smooth and polished throughout, the work of Dunwarr Dwarves who can work stone and metal by feel as much as by sight. The Hearth Road opens into the vast cavern that houses Thelgrim itself, filled with towering stalagmites and outcroppings of living rock. Before the Third Darkness the road ran unbroken into the cavern, but since Gehennor's dragon hybrids used the Hearth Road to reach Thelgrim's heart, the Dwarves have cut a deep chasm across that side of the cavern; only a single arching bridge now links the Hearth Road to the city, guarded at its far end by further gates and battlements. Similar defenses guard the city's northern exit into the Deeps, each set of gates flanked by one of the two barracks of the Warriors' Guild — the southern barracks under Skirmisher Captain Lyssa Svensdottir, who oversees military action outside the mountains, and the northern barracks under her brother Guard Captain Einalf Svensson, who oversees the defense of the Deeps and the Palace Guard protecting King Geirmund Ragnarson.

Thelgrim itself fills a single massive cavern, eternally lit by hundreds of crystal starglobes crafted by the Runescribes' Guild; from outside, their radiance makes the city look like a giant geode, while inside they give the streets a dim, gentle glow that Human visitors tend to find bothersome. Buildings are as often dug directly into the cavern walls and stalagmites as built freestanding on the cavern floor, and are arranged in rings running outward from the city center, where a towering aqueduct redirects an underground river into a Dwarf-crafted lake that also supplies the city's fresh water. At the far end of the lake stands Dunwol Kenn Karnin, "The Fortress inside the Mountain" — a fortress within the fortress-city, from whose depths King Ragnarson rules seated on Tanngnoster's Throne, the final refuge of the Dwarven people in their darkest hours. On the lake's opposite shore stands the Guild Hall, its facade bearing one ornately decorated pillar for each of the ten guilds that form the heart of Dwarven society; unlike the sober royal fortress, the Guild Hall is loud and lively, its guild heads and representatives given to spirited debate that occasionally devolves into brawls ended by dunking the combatants in the lake.

Home to the Runescribes' Guild is the Runeforge, a massive cruciform hall built partly into the cavern walls at the city's edge, housing the Dwarves' collection of known dragon runes. Its four wings each serve a distinct purpose: the south wing holds offices and meeting chambers, the west wing contains the forges, casting molds, and foundries where runic items are made, the north wing houses two copies of every known dragon rune cast in metal and set into rock slabs, and a further wing pointing toward the Molten Heath preserves the complete records of Dwarven history etched onto copper sheets. Beyond preserving that history and etching runes into arms and armor, the runescribes — led by Master Scribe Dorgan Hammerfist — also search for new runes or ones lost with the destruction of the Karok Doum; Hammerfist is said to be considering hiring adventurers to explore the ruins of the Dwarves' ancestral cities on the Molten Heath for that purpose. Deeper still beneath the city's heart lies the Hall of the Ancestors, repository of the Dwarven dead: seven hundred thirty-four unsealed sarcophagi hold the newly deceased for seven hundred thirty-four days before their bones are moved into a miles-long collection of antechambers shared, in a loose sense, with their families or guilds. Only the monarchs of Dunwarr receive individual burials, interred in elaborate sealed tombs whose tableaus and engraved poetry relate the legends of their reigns.
