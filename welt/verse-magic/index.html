Verse Magic

Music has been a part of Mennara's history from its origins; many of the oldest records exist only as songs passed from one generation to the next. When the most talented or earnest pour their hearts into their music, it is as though they can truly cast a spell upon their listeners — for even mundane music echoes in its art the Lays of The First, which flow through and nourish all of creation, and some primal part of every listener remembers the songs of creation.

Description

Each culture has its own traditions of song and music, with different roles for music and musicians depending on their needs: Elven Loremasters sing the histories of their people, relying on tempo and key to convey what words alone cannot; the Dwarves sing ancient rhythmic work songs to keep pace at their crafts; Gnomes use music to lighten the load and lift their spirits while traveling from town to town; Catfolk use song to call the tribes together and communicate while hunting; the Orcish peoples sing to celebrate their ancestors and the spirits of the land that nourish them; and Humans use music to impress and woo one another, or simply to pass the time.

Singers, musicians, dancers, and poets of sufficient skill and sincerity are able to produce magical effects from their melodies, lyrics, and movements; their rhyming structures, vocal ranges, or instrument techniques either evoke the magic of the original Lays or inspire a near-magical response in the listener. Bards need not be trained magicians to accomplish these effects, and indeed most are magically naïve — or boastful — individuals who would credit the effect of their music on their own virtuosity alone. Sorcerers and Runemasters consider bardic magic much more limited and subtle than their own arts, but the troubadour knows the potency of their craft: they can imbue themselves and their companions with improved morale, revitalizing energy, increased determination, feelings of concentration, or speed and stamina on the march, and certain performers can even produce unnerving dissonances that cause fear or resentment in their targets.

The musical instruments of bards are as varied as the lands of Mennara itself. Stringed instruments such as lutes, zithers, and lyres are common in all regions, some renowned Tamalir bards using small dragon scales as picks; bowed instruments like viols and the cranked lyres of Al-Kalim offer richer sounds but require greater skill. Bards who don't rely on their voices favor pipes, flutes, and the like, or drums, chimes, and bell lyres to produce percussive martial tones before battle. Horns are also popular — the Elves employ ones made using materials gifted from sentient creatures of the Aymhelin — but most notorious are Dunwarr bagpipes made from preserved dragonskin, which fables say can turn entire armies away.
